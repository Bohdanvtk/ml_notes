Gaussian Naive Bayes

From the full multivariate Gaussian to a product of 1D Gaussians, step by step, then how to fit and predict.

class Aclass Bprediction

1The decision rule

$$a(x) = \arg\max_{y}\; \lambda_y\, P(y)\, p(x \mid y)$$

\(x = (x^1, \dots, x^n)\) features, \(P(y)\) prior, \(p(x\mid y)\) class density, \(\lambda_y\) cost of a mistake on class \(y\).

2Start: the full Gaussian

$$p(x \mid y) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}\,(\det \Sigma_y)^{1/2}}\; \exp\Bigl\{-\tfrac{1}{2}\,(x-\mu_y)^{T}\,\Sigma_y^{-1}\,(x-\mu_y)\Bigr\}$$

Two hard parts: \(\det \Sigma_y\) and \(\Sigma_y^{-1}\).

3Naive = diagonal covariance

Assumption: features are independent given the class.

$$\Sigma_y = \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_{y,1}^2 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & \sigma_{y,2}^2 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & \sigma_{y,n}^2 \end{pmatrix}$$

Step 1 · determinant = product of the diagonal

$$\det \Sigma_y = \prod_{i=1}^{n} \sigma_{y,i}^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad (\det \Sigma_y)^{1/2} = \prod_{i=1}^{n} \sigma_{y,i}$$

Step 2 · inverse = invert every diagonal entry

$$\Sigma_y^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1/\sigma_{y,1}^2 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & 1/\sigma_{y,2}^2 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & 1/\sigma_{y,n}^2 \end{pmatrix}$$

4The quadratic form becomes a sum

Step 3 · in general

$$(x-\mu_y)^{T}\,\Sigma_y^{-1}\,(x-\mu_y) = \sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n} (x^i-\mu_y^i)\,\bigl[\Sigma_y^{-1}\bigr]_{ij}\,(x^j-\mu_y^j)$$

Diagonal: \(\bigl[\Sigma_y^{-1}\bigr]_{ij} = 0\) for \(i \ne j\), so the cross terms \((x^i-\mu_y^i)(x^j-\mu_y^j)\) vanish.

$$(x-\mu_y)^{T}\,\Sigma_y^{-1}\,(x-\mu_y) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{(x^i-\mu_y^i)^2}{\sigma_{y,i}^2}$$

5A product of 1D Gaussians

Step 4 · exp of a sum = product of exps

$$\exp\Bigl\{-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(x^i-\mu_y^i)^2}{2\sigma_{y,i}^2}\Bigr\} = \prod_{i=1}^{n}\exp\Bigl\{-\frac{(x^i-\mu_y^i)^2}{2\sigma_{y,i}^2}\Bigr\}$$

Step 5 · split the constant

$$(2\pi)^{n/2}\,(\det\Sigma_y)^{1/2} = \prod_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{2\pi}\;\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{y,i} = \prod_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{y,i}^2}$$

Result

$$p(x \mid y) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} \underbrace{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{y,i}^2}}\,\exp\Bigl\{-\frac{(x^i-\mu_y^i)^2}{2\sigma_{y,i}^2}\Bigr\}}_{p(x^i \mid y)\ \text{— a 1D Gaussian}}$$

6Fit

For every class: share of objects, mean and variance of every feature.

$$P(y) = \frac{l_y}{l}, \qquad \mu_y^i = \text{mean of feature } i, \qquad \sigma_{y,i}^2 = \text{mean of } \bigl(x^i - \mu_y^i\bigr)^2$$

Example · 5 objects, 3 of them in class A: \((1,\,2),\ (2,\,5),\ (3,\,2)\)

$$P(A) = \frac{3}{5} = \mathbf{0.6}$$ $$\mu_A^1 = \frac{1+2+3}{3} = \mathbf{2}, \qquad \mu_A^2 = \frac{2+5+2}{3} = \mathbf{3}$$ $$\sigma_{A,1}^2 = \frac{(1-2)^2 + (2-2)^2 + (3-2)^2}{3} = \frac{2}{3} \approx \mathbf{0.67}$$ $$\sigma_{A,2}^2 = \frac{(2-3)^2 + (5-3)^2 + (2-3)^2}{3} = \frac{6}{3} = \mathbf{2}$$

That's the whole training for class A. Same for B.

7Predict

$$a(x) = \arg\max_{y}\; \lambda_y\, P(y) \prod_{i=1}^{n} p(x^i \mid y)$$

Underflow: a product of many small numbers is 0 in floating point.

$$\bigl(10^{-5}\bigr)^{100} = 10^{-500} \;<\; 10^{-308} \approx \text{smallest double}$$

Take \(\ln\): product → sum, argmax unchanged.

$$a(x) = \arg\max_{y}\Bigl[\ln\lambda_y + \ln P(y) + \sum_{i=1}^{n}\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2}\ln\bigl(2\pi\sigma_{y,i}^2\bigr) - \frac{(x^i-\mu_y^i)^2}{2\sigma_{y,i}^2}\Bigr)\Bigr]$$
