Pixels to millimetres

Goal: a phone photo that works as a ruler. Put screws on a printed sheet, take a photo, get “3 mm thick, 20 mm long”. Measuring in pixels is easy; the hard part is the number of pixels in one millimetre.

marker 0marker 1marker 2marker 3

1Obstacle 1 · distance

Raise the phone and the same screw gets smaller on the photo. How many pixels one millimetre is depends on the height \(Z\), which you never know exactly.

$$L_{\text{px}} = \frac{f \cdot L_{\text{mm}}}{Z} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad s = \frac{L_{\text{px}}}{L_{\text{mm}}} = \frac{f}{Z}\ \ \text{px/mm}$$

\(f\) = focal length in pixels (here 3000). Twice as high → half as many pixels per mm.

2Obstacle 2 · tilt

A hand-held phone is always tilted a little. Then the far part of the sheet is deeper than the near part: the rectangle becomes a trapezoid, and the scale is different in every part of the photo.

$$s(u, v) = \frac{f}{Z(u, v)} \qquad Z_{\text{far}} > Z_{\text{near}} \;\Longrightarrow\; s_{\text{far}} < s_{\text{near}}$$

No single “px per mm” is correct for the whole photo.

3Obstacle 3 · lens

The glass bends the picture, most near the edges: straight lines become slight arcs. Phones correct most of it themselves, so what is left is usually small.

$$x_d = x\,(1 + k_1 r^2 + k_2 r^4), \qquad y_d = y\,(1 + k_1 r^2 + k_2 r^4), \qquad r^2 = x^2 + y^2$$

A homography keeps straight lines straight, so it can fix obstacles 1 and 2 but not this one.

4The map: sheet_layout.json

Like four monuments on a city map: we drew the sheet, so we know where every marker is in mm (the json). The detector finds the same corners on the photo in px. Each marker's id says which json row it belongs to.

$$\underbrace{(u_k,\ v_k)}_{\text{photo, px · detector}} \;\longleftrightarrow\; \underbrace{(X_k,\ Y_k)}_{\text{sheet, mm · json}}, \qquad k = 0, 1, 2, 3$$

5From 4 pairs to H

A homography is one 3×3 table that sends any photo pixel to mm on the sheet. It has 8 unknowns; every pair gives 2 equations, so 4 pairs are exactly enough.

The map: multiply, then divide by the third number

$$\begin{bmatrix}x'\\ y'\\ w\end{bmatrix} = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}h_{11} & h_{12} & h_{13}\\ h_{21} & h_{22} & h_{23}\\ h_{31} & h_{32} & 1\end{bmatrix}}_{H}\begin{bmatrix}u\\ v\\ 1\end{bmatrix}, \qquad X = \frac{x'}{w}, \quad Y = \frac{y'}{w}$$

The division by \(w\) is what lets far parts of the photo get a different scale than near parts.

One pair → two linear equations (multiply out the fractions)

$$X = \frac{h_{11}u + h_{12}v + h_{13}}{h_{31}u + h_{32}v + 1} \;\Longrightarrow\; h_{11}u + h_{12}v + h_{13} - h_{31}\,uX - h_{32}\,vX = X$$ $$Y = \frac{h_{21}u + h_{22}v + h_{23}}{h_{31}u + h_{32}v + 1} \;\Longrightarrow\; h_{21}u + h_{22}v + h_{23} - h_{31}\,uY - h_{32}\,vY = Y$$

Stack the rows of the 4 pairs: \(A\,\mathbf h = \mathbf b\) with \(A\) of size 8×8. Solve it once per photo.

6Using H on one point

With H known, every pixel of the photo has a place on the sheet. Send both ends of a screw through H, then measure in mm.

$$\text{pixel } (u, v) \xrightarrow{\ \times H\ } (x', y', w) \xrightarrow{\ \div w\ } (X, Y)\ \text{mm}, \qquad L = \sqrt{(X_B - X_A)^2 + (Y_B - Y_A)^2}$$

7Rectify the whole photo

Apply H to the whole photo and you get a view from straight above: the trapezoid is a rectangle again and the scale is the same everywhere. Obstacles 1 and 2 are gone; only the lens is left.

Warping: every pixel of the top view asks where it came from

$$\text{top-view pixel } (i, j) \;\longrightarrow\; (X, Y) = \Bigl(\frac{i}{10},\ \frac{j}{10}\Bigr)\ \text{mm}$$ $$(X, Y) \;\xrightarrow{\ \times H^{-1},\ \div w\ }\; (u, v) \text{ on the photo} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{copy its colour to } (i, j)$$

10 px = 1 mm, so an A4 sheet becomes 2100 × 2970 px.

8Screw vs paper: blur, Otsu, close

Now measure.py works on the top view (10 px = 1 mm). First goal: a mask where the screw is white (255) and the paper black (0). Three steps; click through them and watch the numbers in the orange box.

Blur: every pixel becomes a weighted mean of its 5×5 neighbours, so lone noisy pixels do not become false islands

$$I_{\text{blur}}(x, y) = \sum_{i=-2}^{2}\sum_{j=-2}^{2} g_i\, g_j\; I(x + i,\ y + j), \qquad g_i \propto e^{-i^2 / (2\sigma^2)},\ \ \sigma = 1.1$$

Otsu: try every threshold T, keep the one that splits the histogram into the two most uniform groups (the valley between the humps)

$$T^{*} = \arg\max_{T}\; \omega_{\text{dark}}(T)\,\omega_{\text{light}}(T)\,\bigl(\mu_{\text{dark}}(T) - \mu_{\text{light}}(T)\bigr)^2$$ $$\text{mask}(x, y) = \begin{cases}255 & I_{\text{blur}}(x, y) \le T^{*}\ \ \text{(screw)}\\ 0 & \text{otherwise (paper)}\end{cases}$$

\(\omega\) = share of pixels in a group, \(\mu\) = its mean brightness. BINARY_INV makes the dark screw white.

Close = dilate, then erode with the same 7 px ellipse (≈ 0.7 mm)

$$\text{close}(m) = \operatorname{erode}\bigl(\operatorname{dilate}(m)\bigr)$$

Dilate: every white blob grows by the kernel radius (3 px), so small black gaps inside it fill in. Erode: everything shrinks back by the same 3 px, so the outer outline returns to its place.

Why: stainless steel shines. The bright stripe is above T, so Otsu calls it paper and the screw falls apart. Closing glues it back without changing the size.

9Contours and the area filter

findContours walks around every white blob, so every screw becomes a separate object. RETR_EXTERNAL keeps only outer outlines. Anything smaller than 200 px² is dust, a print dot or a bit of shadow.

$$200\ \text{px}^2 = \frac{200}{10 \cdot 10}\ \text{mm}^2 = 2\ \text{mm}^2 \;\ll\; \underbrace{\approx 30\ \text{mm}^2}_{\text{smallest screw, M3×10}}$$

10Measuring one screw

Turn the screw horizontal, count white pixels in every column, find where the head ends, then read length and diameter. Everything below is computed live from the mask.

3a · rotate by the angle of the long side of minAreaRect · 3b · profile: one number per column

$$\text{thickness}(x) = \#\{\,y : \text{mask}(x, y) = 255\,\}\ \text{px}, \qquad \frac{\text{px}}{10} = \text{mm}$$

3c · head: on the side whose 1/6 is thicker; it ends at the last column thicker than 1.25 × the typical shaft

$$m = \operatorname{median}\bigl(\text{thickness}[\,0.4n \ldots 0.9n\,]\bigr), \qquad \text{head} = \{\,x : \text{thickness}(x) > 1.25\,m\,\}$$

3d · length and diameter · 3e · nearest standard

$$L = \frac{n}{10}\ \text{mm}, \qquad \varnothing = \frac{P_{95}\bigl(\text{thickness}[\,\text{head end} \ldots 0.95n\,]\bigr)}{10}\ \text{mm}$$ $$\varnothing \to \{3, 4, 5, 6\}\ \ \text{if within } 0.4\ \text{mm}$$ $$L \to \{6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 20, 25, 30\}\ \ \text{if within } 1\ \text{mm}, \qquad \text{otherwise “?”}$$

P95 = “almost the maximum”: the thread crests (the nominal Ø), but not one stray pixel. The mean mixes crests and roots and comes out too small.

11Where it breaks

Each case can be switched on in the widgets above. When it happens, the right answer is “?”, not a wrong size.

Glare wider than 0.7 mm → the gap does not close, the screw falls apart, the profile has a dip. Section 10: “glare 1.0 mm” → Ø ≈ 1.3 mm → “?”.

Shadow of the screw → the shadow is dark, Otsu adds it to the screw, Ø grows. Section 10: “shadow” → Ø ≈ 3.4 mm → “?”.

Two screws touch → one contour around both, measured as one object → “?”. Section 9: “two screws touch”.

Height above the paper → the top of the screw is closer to the camera than the paper the homography was built on, so it looks slightly bigger (parallax). Not simulated here; it grows with the screw diameter and the tilt of the phone.

Debug: after the first photo open debug/<name>_mask.png next to _labeled.jpg to see what the algorithm sees.

12In code

The core of rectify.py. Here H maps straight to top-view pixels, so the mm from the json are multiplied by 10.

import json
import cv2
import numpy as np

layout = json.load(open("sheet_layout.json"))        # marker id -> top-left corner in mm
img = cv2.imread("photo.jpg")
gray = cv2.cvtColor(img, cv2.COLOR_BGR2GRAY)

# 1. find the markers: 4 corners each (TL, TR, BR, BL) in px, plus their ids
aruco = cv2.aruco
detector = aruco.ArucoDetector(aruco.getPredefinedDictionary(aruco.DICT_4X4_50))  # the dictionary the sheet was made with
corners, ids, _ = detector.detectMarkers(gray)

# 2. pairs: top-left corner on the photo (px)  <->  the same corner on the sheet (mm, from the json)
src = np.array([c[0][0] for c in corners], np.float32)
dst = np.array([layout["markers"][str(i)] for i in ids.flatten()], np.float32)

# 3. homography: photo px -> top-view px (10 px = 1 mm)
H, _ = cv2.findHomography(src, dst * 10)

# 4. warp the whole photo into the top view: 210 x 297 mm = 2100 x 2970 px
top = cv2.warpPerspective(img, H, (2100, 2970))

The core of measure.py (sections 8–10), on the top view top.

gray = cv2.cvtColor(top, cv2.COLOR_BGR2GRAY)

# 8. segmentation: screw = 255, paper = 0
blur = cv2.GaussianBlur(gray, (5, 5), 0)
T, mask = cv2.threshold(blur, 0, 255, cv2.THRESH_BINARY_INV + cv2.THRESH_OTSU)
kernel = cv2.getStructuringElement(cv2.MORPH_ELLIPSE, (7, 7))       # ≈ 0.7 mm
mask = cv2.morphologyEx(mask, cv2.MORPH_CLOSE, kernel)

# 9. one contour per screw, drop dust
contours, _ = cv2.findContours(mask, cv2.RETR_EXTERNAL, cv2.CHAIN_APPROX_SIMPLE)
contours = [c for c in contours if cv2.contourArea(c) >= 200]       # 2 mm²

for c in contours:
    # 10a. rotate so the long side of the box is horizontal
    rect = cv2.minAreaRect(c)
    box = cv2.boxPoints(rect)
    edges = [box[(i + 1) % 4] - box[i] for i in range(4)]
    dx, dy = max(edges, key=np.linalg.norm)
    angle = np.degrees(np.arctan2(dy, dx))
    one = np.zeros_like(mask)
    cv2.drawContours(one, [c], -1, 255, cv2.FILLED)
    R = cv2.getRotationMatrix2D(rect[0], angle, 1.0)
    rot = cv2.warpAffine(one, R, one.shape[::-1], flags=cv2.INTER_NEAREST)

    # 10b. thickness profile: white pixels per column
    prof = np.count_nonzero(rot, axis=0)
    prof = prof[prof > 0]
    n = len(prof)

    # 10c. head side and head end
    k = max(1, n // 6)
    if prof[-k:].mean() > prof[:k].mean():
        prof = prof[::-1]                                                # head on the left
    med = np.median(prof[int(0.4 * n):int(0.9 * n)])
    thick = np.nonzero(prof[:int(0.4 * n)] > 1.25 * med)[0]
    head_end = thick[-1] + 1 if len(thick) else 0

    # 10d. length and diameter, 10 px = 1 mm
    L = n / 10
    D = np.percentile(prof[head_end:int(0.95 * n)], 95) / 10
